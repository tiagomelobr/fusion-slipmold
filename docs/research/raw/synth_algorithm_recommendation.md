Build undercut detection and parting in stages, from simple (revolved) to general. A deterministic geometric test is always the source of truth, and pass/fail thresholds come only from parameters.

STAGE A (v1): REVOLVED FAST PATH. Exact, needs no ray casting.
1. Intake checks the body is axisymmetric. Either it comes from a RevolveFeature, or all faces are surfaces of revolution about one axis.
2. Get the outer profile r(z) on a half-plane through the axis. Use TemporaryBRepManager.planeIntersection, or the revolve sketch's curves (read-only). Scale by wareScale. Append the spare profile: step-out, height, flare.
3. Classify each height (Chen & McMains 2007):
   a. Single-valued r(z) with the solid reaching the axis: every horizontal slice is a disk. Any axial plane gives undercut-free side halves, even across necks and bulges.
   b. Annular slices, where a horizontal line crosses the boundary more than twice (foot recess, foot ring, downward-folded lip): those z-ranges must belong to a piece pulled along Z. The bottom piece takes them up to bottomSplitHeight = top of the highest annular slice + bottomSplitMargin, snapped to an existing circular edge within reach. A top or spare ring takes them near the rim.
   c. r(z) non-decreasing from the foot to the spare top, with the base recess drafted at least coreDraftMinDeg: a one-piece drop-out mold is feasible (if allowDropOut).
   d. Any face not generated by the revolve (handle, spout, emboss) disables this path. It is routed to a separate mold (CER-08) or to Stage C.
4. Choose a layout: drop-out if feasible and allowed. Otherwise sides2Bottom (the user's current 3-piece mug). sidePieceCount 3 or 4 only if the user wants more draft at the seams (30 or 45 deg instead of 0 on vertical walls). Choose splitAzimuth for handles or bed fit, since it does not affect a pure revolve.
5. Report: z-ranges per piece, zero-draft area, seam lengths, and predicted spare drop. Approval gate.

STAGE B: MESH VERIFICATION. Always run, including on revolved forms; this catches modeling surprises.
1. TriangleMeshCalculator on the cast plug (ware + spare): surfaceTolerance 0.005 cm, maxSideLength about sqrt(2.3 x area / 20000).
2. Pure Python, per pull direction d. Rotate so d = +Z, bucket triangles in a 60 x 60 grid, then run:
   a. Span test: a raster of lines parallel to d. More than 2 crossings means the body is not a double height field along d.
   b. Per-triangle draft: n.d against sin(undercutTolDeg) and sin(draftWarnDeg).
   c. Occlusion: is a front-facing triangle hidden by another triangle farther along d (depth tolerance 0.1 mm)?
   Measured cost is 0.17-0.52 s per direction at 7.6k-21k triangles. Keep each MCP call to about 20 directions or fewer.
3. Label each sample with its piece in the chosen layout: z below the split goes to the bottom (pull -Z); otherwise the azimuth sector picks a side, pulled along the sector bisector. The open top cap is excluded.
4. Pass criterion: undercut area = 0 (no residual tolerance), all cores at least coreDraftMinDeg. Report the zero-draft area. Measured on the user's mug: 114 of 6,400 X-direction rays crossed more than twice, all in the 3.45 mm foot-recess band. That confirms the bottom piece.

STAGE C (v2): GENERAL LAYOUT SEARCH FOR NON-REVOLVED VESSELS. Gated on validating the gap-5 rules.
1. Precompute ACCESS[i, j] for about 5k area-weighted samples against a fixed direction set: horizontal every 2 deg plus +Z and -Z, and for freeform shapes 200-500 Fibonacci-sphere directions plus clustered normals and principal axes.
2. Enumerate layouts in order: dropOut, sides2, sides2Bottom, sides3Bottom, sides4Bottom, then add a spare ring. Sweep phi0 in 2 deg steps over [0, 360/K) and bottomSplitHeight over candidate heights taken from slice analysis.
3. Score each candidate by lookup: undercut area (must be 0), zero-draft area, piece count, seam length, and a seam-on-edge bonus.
4. Return the simplest zero-undercut layout, plus alternatives and an undercut heat map in the JSON result. Show it with a single screenshot only if the user needs the visual.
5. If no layout works, propose fixes. Never apply one silently:
   - cast the appendage separately
   - add a piece
   - fill or modify the undercut on the ware (with the user's approval)
   - reorient
6. Slices give a cheap 2D check for faceted or lobed forms: lines parallel to d cross each horizontal section loop at most twice.

STAGE D (later): NON-PLANAR PARTING. Only if planar layouts fail on important forms.
- Get silhouettes from TemporaryBRepManager.createSilhouetteCurves (measured: 0.02 s for 13 faces) or SilhouetteSplit FacesOnly.
- Compute flatness psi. If psi is at least 0.98 and the deviation is at most 0.5 mm, use a planar split.
- Otherwise build a ruled run-off surface (RuledSurfaceFeatures, direction type) and split the plaster with it. Casings for non-planar seams need separate design work.

POST-BUILD VERIFICATION (all stages). Read-only, on the real pieces:
1. Virtual demold: copy each piece with TemporaryBRep, translate it 0.01/0.1/1.0 cm along its pull, and intersect with the cast and the remaining pieces. The volume must be below 0.001 cm3.
2. Search disassembly orders (at most 5! for 5 pieces). Each natch axis must be parallel to the relative separation direction of its two pieces. In the default order (spare, then bottom with the mold inverted, then sides), natches on the side/bottom seam have a Z axis and natches on the side/side seam have an X axis.
3. Interference: none. Volume balance within 0.1 %. Wall-thickness ratio check: warn below 0.8x, fail below 0.6x or 15 mm.
4. Per-piece weights and plaster batch.
5. Repeat the release logic for each casing part against its plaster piece (PRN-07).

Performance rules: never put bulk work through findBRepUsingRay, which costs 0.15 ms per call and returns only the first hit per face. Keep the triangle budget near 20k. Run analysis with readOnly: true. Keep every call under 30 s. No numpy.
